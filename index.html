About

An engineer working between the brain and the body.

I completed my Ph.D. in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Florida in the Human Neuromechanics Laboratory under Daniel Ferris. My doctoral work was part of a large NIH U01 study (Mind in Motion) that investigated how aging changes the neural and biomechanical control of walking. I led EEG data collection across older and younger adults and developed methods to relate cortical dynamics to gait kinematics.

I now serve as Director of Health Professions Simulations at Lake-Sumter State College, where I build and run simulation facilities — high-fidelity manikins, virtual reality, and AI-mediated patient encounters — that train nursing, respiratory therapy, and medical laboratory students. I am especially interested in how immersive technology and AI agents can help clinicians develop the soft skills that traditional training under-emphasizes.

My research bridges neuroscience, signal processing, biomechanics, and clinical translation. I am happiest at the intersection: a problem that is part rigorous data analysis and part real human consequence.

Projects

Things I'm building right now.

A working list of current and recent work spanning clinical simulation, neural engineering methods, and applied AI for healthcare training.

Active 2026 –

AI-mediated soft-skill training for nursing simulation

Designing simulation scenarios where AI agents play standardized patients in mental-health and complex care contexts, so students can practice de-escalation, motivational interviewing, and bedside manner before working with real patients. Evaluating learning outcomes against traditional manikin-based scenarios.

Active 2025 –

Health Professions simulation facility design

Coordinating the buildout of a new multi-discipline simulation space at Lake-Sumter State College — integrating audio-visual capture, VR training stations, and high-fidelity manikins, and writing the SOPs that bring the program to accreditation standards.

Active 2024 –

Stride-by-stride EEG variability in aging

Continuing the doctoral work on electrocortical dynamics of walking — quantifying how interstride power spectra in theta and beta bands relate to mediolateral gait variability across the lifespan. Manuscript in revision at IEEE TNSRE.

Past 2021 – 2022

Embedded fall detection — Lively Mobile2

With Best Buy Health, engineered a barometer-derived feature for an embedded random-forest fall-detection model, contributing to a ~20% improvement in detection sensitivity on wrist-and-chest IMU data from older adults.

Past 2020 – 2025

Mind in Motion — NIH U01 EEG cohort

Led EEG collection across the older- and younger-adult cohorts of the NIH U01 Mind in Motion study, contributing to four published or in-revision papers on the neural control of human walking.

Past 2021 – 2024

Outreach — Girls With Nerve & Biomechanics Day

Built and ran neuroscience and biomechanics demos for middle-school students, including a VR-on-a-tightrope balance demonstration that became a Biomechanics Day favorite.
