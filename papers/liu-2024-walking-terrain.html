Electrical Brain Activity during Human Walking with Parametric Variations in Terrain Unevenness and Walking Speed

Liu C, Downey RJ, Salminen JS, Rojas SA, Richer N, Pliner EM, Hwang J, Cruz-Almeida Y, Manini TM, Hass CJ, Seidler RD, Clark DJ, Ferris DP

Venue · Imaging Neuroscience Vol. · 2, 1–33 (2024) DOI · 10.1162/imag_a_00097
Read on publisher ↗

The plain-language version

Real-world walking varies along two main axes: how fast you go, and how rough the ground is. Most prior EEG-during-walking work has changed only one at a time. This paper crosses both, parametrically, and maps how the cortex responds across the full grid.

Younger adults walked on a treadmill that independently varied speed and terrain unevenness. We recorded high-density EEG, decomposed it into source-localized cortical clusters, and quantified how power in each cluster scaled with each axis — separately and together.

Why this matters

This is the foundational study that establishes a reference grid for everything our group does next. Once you know how the healthy younger cortex scales with speed and terrain, you can ask the harder questions about aging, balance disorders, and mobility decline against a known baseline.

My contribution

Co-led EEG data collection alongside the rest of the Mind in Motion team and contributed to writing, revisions, and analysis.
