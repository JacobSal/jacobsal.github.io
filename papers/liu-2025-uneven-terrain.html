Age Differences in Electrocortical Dynamics During Uneven Terrain Walking

Liu C, Pliner EM, Salminen J, Downey RJ, Hwang J, Roy A, Swearinger R, Richer N, Hass CJ, Clark DJ, Manini TM, Cruz-Almeida Y, Seidler RD, Ferris DP

Venue · Imaging Neuroscience Vol. · 3, IMAG.a.1039 (2025) DOI · 10.1162/IMAG.a.1039
Read on publisher ↗

The plain-language version

Walking on uneven ground is more cognitively demanding than walking on a flat treadmill — you have to plan foot placement, monitor balance, and react to the surface in real time. This paper asks how the brain handles that extra demand, and whether older adults' brains handle it differently from younger adults'.

Participants walked on a treadmill that mechanically varied terrain unevenness while we recorded high-density EEG and gait kinematics. The analysis isolates cortical sources whose activity scales with how uneven the surface is, and compares those scaling patterns between younger and older groups.

Why this matters

Falls are a leading source of injury and disability in older adults, and they happen in real-world environments that look nothing like a treadmill belt. If we can show that the cortical signals controlling walking are reorganized in aging — particularly under increased terrain demand — we get a much sharper picture of where mobility decline begins, and a target for interventions.

My contribution

Co-led EEG data collection across the older- and younger-adult cohorts of the NIH U01 Mind in Motion study, which provided the dataset for this analysis, and contributed to writing, revisions, of analyses.
